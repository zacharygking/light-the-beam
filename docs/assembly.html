Part 1 · Print the parts

Get the files and measure the arena

1

Download the arena from Printables

5 min
  1. Open printables.com/model/338758 and sign in (a free account is required to download).
  2. Click Download, then in the file list take only the two files for the 220 mm size: the base and the lid with no hole. Skip the 190 and 296 mm files and the pen-holder remix.
  3. Save both into the project folder models/printables/.
Why the no-hole lid? The author's hole is sized for a store-bought light bar. Our beam tube is 12 mm, so you'll cut the hole yourself in step 3. If you'd rather not, download the holed lid too and measure it; if it's 12–13 mm it will work.
2

Measure the base for the plinth

10 min

The plinth is generated to fit your arena, so it needs two numbers from the base file.

  1. Open Bambu Studio, drag the base STL onto the plate. Click the model and check the size readout in the right panel: you should see roughly 220 × 200 × 70 mm.
  2. Choose the Measure tool (ruler icon in the top toolbar). Click the flat back edge of the footprint, then the front prow: that's the depth. Then the widest left–right span: the width. Write both down.
  3. Look at the back wall near the floor for the author's wire hole. Note which side it's on when the flat edge faces you: that is the back, angle 180°.
  4. Open models/plinth.scad in a text editor. Set arena_d to the larger of your two numbers, and arena_wire_a to 180 (back) or wherever the hole is. Save.
Your arena_d should be within a few mm of 220. If it's wildly different you picked the wrong size file.
3

Make the plinth, tube, spine and socket files

5 min
  1. Install OpenSCAD if you don't have it (free, openscad.org, drag to Applications).
  2. In Terminal: cd models && make. Six STLs appear in models/exports/. The plinth takes a minute to render; that's normal.
cd ~/dev/personal/prints/sacramento_kings_beam/models
make            # writes exports/plinth.stl, plinth_lid.stl, plinth_test.stl, beam_tube.stl, beam_spine.stl, beam_socket.stl

Part 1 · Print the parts

Slice and print

4

Print the plinth test slice first

40 min print
  1. In Bambu Studio, import exports/plinth_test.stl. It's a 64 mm wide slice of the plinth's front. Print it in silver PLA, 0.2 mm, default walls, flat face down.
  2. When it's done, slide the display board H into the pocket from the open bottom, screen toward the window. It should slide with light finger pressure and stop with the glass against the window lip.
  3. Look at it from the front. The picture area of the screen (not the black border) should be centred in the opening with an even margin.
Board slides in, screen centred, no rattle. If it's too tight, raise pocket_clear in plinth.scad by 0.2. If the picture is off to one side, change screen_ofs_x by the amount it's off (in mm; positive moves the window right). Re-run make and reprint only the test slice.
5

Cut the beam hole in the lid

3 min
  1. Import the no-hole lid. Right-click it → Add Negative Part → Cylinder.
  2. In the negative part's settings set size 12.4 × 12.4 × 20 mm and position it dead centre of the lid (X 0, Y 0 relative to the lid), sticking through the top and bottom.
  3. Slice and confirm the preview shows a round hole through the lid.
12.4, not 12. The tube is 12 mm; the extra 0.4 is print tolerance so it slides without forcing.
6

Print everything

Plate layout. The base alone fills most of the plate. Print it on its own, then lid + spine + socket together, then the plinth on its own, then the tube on its own (a 250 mm vase print doesn't like neighbours).

Part 2 · Flash the board

Put the firmware on the display board

Do this before assembly, while the board is easy to plug in. You only ever do it once; WiFi is set up from your phone later.

7

Install PlatformIO (once)

5 min
  1. Open Terminal and run the command below. It installs the build tool into your user folder; nothing system-wide changes.
python3 -m pip install --user platformio
export PATH="$HOME/Library/Python/3.9/bin:$PATH"     # add this line to ~/.zshrc so it sticks
8

Connect the board and flash the demo

5 min
  1. Plug the board H into your Mac with its USB cable. Use the micro-USB port (some boards also have USB-C; either works for flashing).
  2. In Terminal, go to the firmware folder and flash the demo build first. The first build downloads the ESP32 toolchain (a few hundred MB) and takes a few minutes.
cd ~/dev/personal/prints/sacramento_kings_beam/firmware
pio run -e cyd_demo -t upload
  1. Watch the end of the output for SUCCESS. The board restarts by itself.
The screen lights up and cycles every 9 seconds: NEXT GAME (logos, countdown) → LIVE (big score) → FINAL · LIGHT THE BEAM. Tap the screen: nothing visible yet (no strip), but the touch is being read.
If the upload fails with "no serial port": unplug, plug back in, run again. If it still fails, hold the board's BOOT button while the upload starts, release after "Connecting…". If the picture is upside down or the colours are inverted, see Troubleshooting on page 13 and reflash.
9

Flash the real firmware

3 min
pio run -e cyd -t upload
The screen shows the Kings logo, "STARTING", then WIFI SETUP with four instructions. That's correct: it has no WiFi yet. Unplug the board; you'll set up WiFi in Part 5.
Want to watch it think? pio device monitor -b 115200 shows the log: WiFi, each ESPN fetch, and the beam decisions.

Part 3 · Build the beam

Cut and mount the strip

10

Cut the strip to 15 LEDs

5 min
  1. Unroll the LED strip I. One end has three wires already attached (red, green, white or black) and usually a small white plug. That end stays.
  2. Look closely at the strip: small arrows are printed along it. They point away from the wired end. Data travels in the arrow direction; the wired end is the input.
  3. Starting at the wired end, count 15 LEDs. Put a piece of masking tape after the 15th.
  4. Between LED 15 and LED 16 there is a set of three copper pads with a line through the middle. Cut exactly on that line with scissors, straight across.
  5. Put the rest of the roll away; it's spare.
Cut on the line, not through an LED. Cutting anywhere else kills the LED on each side of the cut. If you slip, cut on the next line and live with 14 LEDs; the firmware doesn't mind.
11

Stick the strip to the spine

5 min
  1. Take spine F. It's a flat white bar with a small notch at one end. The notch is the bottom.
  2. Wipe the spine with the alcohol wipe and let it dry; the strip's adhesive needs a clean surface.
  3. Peel 3 cm of the strip's paper backing. Line the strip up on the flat face of the spine so the wired end is at the notch and the three wires exit through it.
  4. Press down the first few LEDs, then peel and press a bit at a time along the length, keeping the strip straight and centred. It should end about 10 mm short of the top.
  5. Rub the whole length down with a thumb.
Wires exit at the notched bottom, LEDs face away from the spine, strip runs straight to near the top.

Part 3 · Build the beam

Into the tube, and a first light

12

Slide the spine into the tube

3 min
  1. Hold tube E with the wide foot flange down. The tube is open at the foot.
  2. Feed the top of the spine (the end without wires) into the foot opening and push it up, LEDs facing the tube wall (any direction; it's round).
  3. Push until the small feet at the bottom of the spine stop against the inside of the flange. The three wires should hang out of the foot.
Stuck halfway? The single-wall tube can be slightly oval. Rotate the spine 90° and try again, or gently squeeze the tube round. Never force it; the wall is 0.45 mm.
Optional: one small dab of hot glue where a spine foot meets the flange stops the spine sliding out when you tip the tube. Keep glue off the strip.
13

Test the beam before it goes inside the arena

5 min

It's much easier to fix a wiring mistake now than after the glue.

  1. Find the two small pigtail leads that came with board H: thin cables with a tiny white plug on one end and bare tinned wires on the other. Plug one into the 4-pin socket labelled P1 (near the USB port) and one into CN1 (the 4-pin socket on the edge).
  2. Identify the wires using the printed labels next to each socket. You need three: VIN and GND from P1, and IO22 from CN1. Put a bit of tape on each and write the name on it. Fold any unused wires back and tape them so they can't touch anything.
  3. Open the three lever connectors J (flip the orange levers up). Make three joints, one per connector, matching the table on page 10: VIN ↔ red, GND ↔ white/black, IO22 ↔ green. Push each bare wire in until it stops, then close the lever. The board's pigtail wires are thin: fold the bare end back on itself once so it's doubled before inserting.
  4. Tug each wire gently. None should pull out.
  5. Plug the board into the USB adapter K. It boots to WIFI SETUP. Tap the screen once.
The strip lights purple from the bottom LED up, then all 15 hold purple with a slow shimmer. Tap again: they go dark. Hold your finger on the screen for a second: brightness steps down; hold twice more: back to full.
Nothing lights? Unplug first. Check green is on IO22 (not IO27) and red is on VIN (not 3V3). Check the strip's arrows point away from the wired end. Only the first LED lights, or colours flicker? The data signal is marginal: shorten the green lead, or add the optional 74AHCT125 level shifter between IO22 and the strip.

Part 4 · Arena and plinth

Mount the beam in the arena

14

Glue the socket to the arena floor

5 min + glue time
  1. Unplug the board and open the three lever nuts to free the strip. Leave the taped board leads in place.
  2. Take arena base A and find the centre of the floor: measure the width and the depth of the inside floor with the ruler, mark the halfway point of each with a pencil, and mark where they cross.
  3. Wipe the floor there and the underside of socket G with the alcohol wipe.
  4. Socket G has a slot in its side. Sit it dry on the mark with the slot facing the back of the arena (the flat edge, where the wire hole is). Draw round it with the pencil.
  5. Lift it, run a ring of hot glue on its underside (or four dots of super glue), and press it back onto the pencil outline. Hold for 30 seconds. Super glue: leave it 10 minutes.
Centre and level matter. The tube is 250 mm tall, so a socket 2 mm off-centre puts the tube off-centre in the lid hole, and a socket glued on a blob of glue makes the beam lean. Press it flat.
15

Stand the beam and route the wires

3 min
  1. Pass the strip's three wires through the socket's slot, then along the floor and out through the wire hole in the back wall. Pull them through so there's no slack coiled inside.
  2. Lower the tube's foot flange into the socket. It should drop in with a little play and stand straight up.
  3. Sight down from the front and from the side: the tube should be vertical. If it leans, the socket isn't flat; reheat the glue and reseat.
Tube stands straight, spins freely in the socket, wires exit the back of the arena with no strain on the strip.

Part 4 · Arena and plinth

Fit the lid, seat the board

16

Fit the lid

1 min
  1. Hold lid B level above the arena, hole over the tube. Lower it straight down over the tube until it rests on the arena's rim.
  2. Turn it until the roof's flat back edge lines up with the arena's flat back edge.
The lid sits flat all round and the tube passes through the hole without touching its edge. If it touches, the socket is off-centre (step 14) or the hole isn't centred (step 5).
17

Seat the display board in the plinth

5 min
  1. Turn plinth C upside down on the table. Behind the front window you'll see the rectangular pocket; it's open toward you (the bottom).
  2. Unplug the two pigtails from board H for a moment so nothing snags. Hold the board with the screen facing the window and the USB port toward the bottom edge of the plinth (toward you).
  3. Slide the board into the pocket until the glass rests against the window lip. It's the same fit you tested in step 4.
  4. Plug the two pigtails back into P1 and CN1. Plug the USB cable into the board.
  5. Turn the plinth right side up and look at the screen through the window: the picture area should be centred.
Loose? A folded strip of masking tape on the back of the board (not the screen) takes up slack. The bottom lid's bump also presses the board up in step 18.

Part 4 · Arena and plinth

Connect the strip and close it up

18

Make the three connections (for real this time)

5 min

Same three joints as the test in step 13, now with the strip inside the arena and the board inside the plinth.

Board pigtailStrip wireCarriesConnector
VIN (P1)Red5 V powerJ1
GND (P1)White (or black)GroundJ2
IO22 (CN1)GreenDataJ3
  1. Set the arena on the table behind the plinth, with its wire hole facing the plinth's back.
  2. Feed the strip's three wires down through the slot in the plinth's top plate at the back, into the plinth.
  3. Through the open bottom of the plinth, make the three lever-nut joints per the table. Fold the thin board wires double as before. Close each lever firmly; you'll feel it click.
  4. Tug-test all six wire ends.
Red goes to VIN, never to 3V3. 3V3 can't supply the strip and you'll get a dim, flickering beam. Green goes to IO22, not IO27.
19

Route the cable and close the plinth

5 min
  1. Tuck the three connectors into the plinth, toward the back, away from the board.
  2. Route the board's USB cable out the slot at the back of the plinth (or, if you fitted the optional panel jack, plug its short cable into the board and leave the jack's socket facing out).
  3. Fit bottom lid D: line up its finger hole toward the back, press it in evenly until it's flush. The bump on the lid lands under the board and pushes it up against the window.
  4. Set the arena down onto the plinth. It drops into the shallow recess in the top plate; turn it so the flat back edge is at the back and the strip wires aren't pinched.
Plinth lid flush, arena seated with no rocking, one cable out the back. Looking in the front window, the screen is centred and straight.

Part 5 · Power on and set up

Join WiFi and light the beam

20

Power on

1 min
  1. Plug the USB cable into adapter K and the adapter into the wall.
  2. The screen shows the Kings logo and STARTING, then CONNECTING TO WIFI for up to 20 seconds, then WIFI SETUP.
Use the 2 A adapter, not a laptop port. The beam at full brightness plus the board draws about 1 A; a laptop port may brown out and restart the board mid-animation.
21

Connect it to your home WiFi from your phone

3 min
  1. On your phone, open Settings → WiFi. A new network called KingsBeam-Setup appears (it can take 20 seconds). Join it. There's no password.
  2. A setup page pops up on its own, like a hotel login page. If nothing appears within 15 seconds, open the browser and type 192.168.4.1 in the address bar.
  3. Tap Configure WiFi. The page lists nearby networks. Tap your home network. If it isn't listed, tap Refresh; the board only sees 2.4 GHz networks, so pick the 2.4 GHz name if your router has separate ones.
  4. Type your WiFi password carefully (it's case-sensitive; tap the eye icon to check it) and tap Save.
  5. The setup network disappears; your phone reconnects to your normal WiFi by itself. The board's screen shows CONNECTED and then, within about 10 seconds, NEXT GAME with the opponent and tipoff time.
The NEXT GAME screen shows the right opponent and a sensible countdown. Time is synced from the internet, so the tipoff shown is already in Pacific time.
Wrong password? The board tries for 20 seconds, then opens KingsBeam-Setup again. Just repeat from step 1. iPhone won't show the setup page? Turn off cellular data for a minute so the phone stays on the setup network, or use the address above.
22

Light the beam

1 min
  1. Tap the screen once. Purple climbs the tube from the base over two seconds and holds. Tap again to turn it off.
  2. Hold a finger on the screen for a second to step the brightness (three levels).
Beam rises evenly with no dark LEDs. You're done. It will light itself the next time the Kings win.

Day to day

Using it

What the screen shows

Next gameOpponent logo, VS (home) or @ (away), tipoff in Pacific time, countdown pill. Refreshes every 10 minutes; every 30 seconds in the last 20 minutes before tipoff.
LiveScore, quarter and clock, refreshed every 20 seconds. Leader in white, trailer in grey with a marker under the leader. Halftime, end of quarter and overtime are labelled. The beam pulses softly.
Final, Kings winScore, "LIGHT THE BEAM", and the beam rises and holds.
Final, lossScore and "NEXT TIME". The beam stays off unless you tap it.
No game scheduledOff-season: the Kings logo and "NO GAME SCHEDULED".
OfflineA small OFFLINE badge in the corner. The last good screen stays; it retries every minute.

Touch

TapBeam on / off, in any state.
Hold 1 sCycle beam brightness: low → medium → full.

Automatic behaviour

After a winThe beam stays lit up to 12 hours, until you tap it off, or until the next game shows up on the schedule.
At nightScreen dims 11 pm–7 am, and whenever the room is dark (the board has a light sensor).
Power lossReboots into the current state in ~15 seconds; WiFi is remembered.
New WiFiIf it can't find the saved network for 20 seconds after boot, KingsBeam-Setup opens again. Repeat step 21.

Care

Dust with a dry cloth. Keep out of direct sun and off radiators: PLA softens around 55 °C and the tube is thin. The lid lifts off to reach the beam; the bottom lid pulls off (finger hole) to reach the board. Unplug before opening.

If something's off

Troubleshooting

SymptomTry
Screen stays darkUse the 2 A adapter, not a laptop. Try the board's other USB port if it has two. Check the cable is a data cable if you're flashing.
Colours inverted or milkyYou have the other board variant. In firmware/platformio.ini, comment out ILI9341_2_DRIVER and enable the ST7789_DRIVER + TFT_INVERSION_ON lines noted there; reflash.
Picture upside downIn firmware/src/ui.cpp change LV_DISPLAY_ROTATION_90 to _270; reflash.
Screen off-centre in the windowAdjust screen_ofs_x / screen_ofs_y in models/plinth.scad by the offset in mm, make, reprint the test slice.
Beam never lights, even on tapUnplug. Check red → VIN (not 3V3), green → IO22 (not IO27), white → GND. Check the strip's arrows point away from the wired end. Check each lever nut grips (tug test).
Only LED 1 lights / random coloursData signal is marginal. Shorten the green lead to the minimum, or add the 74AHCT125 level shifter between IO22 and the strip's green wire.
Beam dim or flickers with the screenWeak power. Use a 2 A adapter and a short USB cable. Step brightness down (hold 1 s) if it's a weak charger you can't change.
Beam leansSocket not flat or off-centre: lift the lid, reheat the glue, reseat (step 14).
OFFLINE badge won't clearESPN unreachable from your network. It retries every minute. Check other devices have internet; reboot the board. If it persists for days, ESPN may have changed its edge rules; check python3 tools/espn_probe.py on your Mac.
Countdown wrong by hoursClock not synced yet; wait a minute after connecting. If it stays wrong, your router blocks NTP (UDP 123).
Taps ignoredPress firmly with a fingertip in the middle of the screen; it's a resistive panel. Lower Z_THRESHOLD in firmware/src/touch.cpp if it never registers.
Setup network never appearsPower-cycle the board. It only opens the portal when it can't connect; if it already knows a WiFi and that WiFi is up, there's nothing to set up.

Safety

Low-voltage USB only; never connect to anything but a 5 V USB adapter. Indoor use. Unplug before opening the plinth or touching the wiring. The beam tube is thin-walled and not a toy. Printed PLA parts are not food-safe and should be kept away from heat.
